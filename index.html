    • Windows: install loopMIDI, create a port, pick it above.

    How it worksReal sensors, the same rule every time, no active AI

    Live earthquakes, solar wind, ocean swell, tides and the space station, played as sound and sent as MIDI. Every note is a real event: a quake, a solar flare, the sun rising over Lagos. Filter sweeps follow the actual solar wind, the drone bends with real tides. Listen, play along, or take the raw data into your DAW.

    1. Instruments measureseismometers, satellites, tide gauges, weather balloons
    2. Agencies publishUSGS, NOAA, NASA, Open-Meteo
    3. Your browser fetchesas published, no edits
    4. One rule per soundprinted on every card, same reading = same note
    5. Oscillators playWeb Audio synthesis, built live
    6. You producelisten, record, or export the data
    So what turns a reading into sound?
    1 · ReadingUSGS: M5.1 quake10 km deep · 142°E
    2 · Rulevelocity = 20 + (mag − 1) ÷ 6 × 107
    shallower → higher note
    longitude → left/right
    3 · SoundBell at velocity 93high note · panned right · rings ~6 s

    That rule is a few lines of plain code, like a synth patch, written once. Nothing listens, learns or improvises. Feed it the same reading tomorrow and you get the same note. Every card below prints its own rule, and tapping any event in the log shows its full chain.

    Where AI was used: building, not playing
    Claude (an AI) helped Colin design and build this site, including the rules that turn each reading into sound. That all happened before you got here. No AI runs while you listen: no model picks a note, makes a sound or changes a rule. Don't take our word for it: the ledger below lists every server this page talks to, live, and the code is public.

      Replay compresses the last 24 hours, keeping every event in its real order and spacing. The clock shows the real UTC time being played. Live mode plays new quakes within about a minute of USGS publishing them. Solar wind is measured at L1, 30–60 minutes before it reaches Earth. The ISS position is propagated from its published orbital elements and checked against a live fix (the gap is shown on its card). Sunrise times are computed. If any source fails, the status bar says demo for that feed and nothing is passed off as live.
